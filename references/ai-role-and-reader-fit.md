# AI role and reader fit

Line editing is too late when AI already chose the center, structure, and emotional shape. Check the role before cleaning the prose.

## Role check

Ask three questions:

1. Who chose the core claim?
2. Who chose the outline and first example?
3. Did the writer react to a live source, event, or reader need before AI drafted?

If the human supplied the claim and structure, AI can critique and edit. If AI supplied the first shape, run the anti-anchoring pass before line edits.

## Anti-anchoring pass

1. Put the current draft aside.
2. Write the core point in one sentence without borrowing the draft's nouns or order.
3. List the evidence, uncertainty, and source boundaries that must survive.
4. Choose the reader's needed action or understanding.
5. Rebuild the outline from those constraints.
6. Reuse a line from the AI draft only when it still fits the new center.

## Reader-fit check

For public, promotional, social, founder, or expert writing, check:

- **Trigger:** Does the text answer a specific event, claim, question, or tension?
- **Stance:** Can the reader find the writer's judgment or decision?
- **Evidence:** Is every strong claim sourced, qualified, or clearly framed as opinion?
- **Specificity:** Does the text name the actor, object, constraint, or result?
- **Channel:** Does the rhythm fit the destination instead of copying generic social texture?
- **Residue:** Did the final pass remove formulaic words and repeated structures without sanding away the writer's voice?

Do not turn this list into a fake precision score. Use it to find the first concrete failure.

## Source boundaries

Never improve a vague claim by inventing a number, timeline, actor, mechanism, or causal link.

When the draft lacks support:

1. keep the uncertainty visible
2. name the missing source
3. narrow the claim to what the draft supports
4. stop and ask for evidence when stronger wording depends on it

## Critique-before-rewrite prompt

```text
Step 1. Diagnose without rewriting.
Find AI-first anchoring, model-average claims, stale platform cues, reader-fit failures, unsupported confidence, and missing source context.

Step 2. Check the sources.
List what must be verified before any claim becomes stronger. Do not invent evidence.

Step 3. Choose the human center.
State the writer's claim, evidence boundary, and intended reader effect.

Step 4. Plan the rewrite.
Name the structural moves before drafting.

Step 5. Rewrite and run Stop Slop.
Return the clean prose first. Keep the diagnosis backstage unless review is needed.
```
