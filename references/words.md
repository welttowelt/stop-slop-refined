See SKILL.md for usage.

# Word tiers

Three house-rule tiers. Use them to prioritize edits, not to identify who wrote the text. Each entry covers its inflected forms (delve, delving, delved). Tell words rotate by model era, so run the refresh loop in SKILL.md (Catalog refresh) instead of trusting this list to stay current on its own.

- **Tier 1 — Replace by default.** Keep only when required by a technical term, source quotation, or proper name.
- **Tier 2 — Check in clusters.** One use may be fine. Repeated use in a paragraph needs attention.
- **Tier 3 — Check at density.** Keep precise uses. Replace repeated vague uses with facts.

Technical-writing exemption. In dev docs and technical explanations, robust, comprehensive, seamless, ecosystem, leverage (the platform or API sense), facilitate, underpin, and streamline are legitimate working terms. Keep them there. Delve, tapestry, beacon, embark, testament to, and game-changer stay flagged everywhere.

## Tier 3 — Flag only at high density

| Word | Action |
|---|---|
| significant / significantly | Replace some with specifics: numbers, comparisons |
| innovative / innovation | Describe what's actually new |
| effective / effectively | Say how, or cite a metric |
| dynamic / dynamics | Name the actual forces |
| scalable / scalability | Describe what scales and to what |

| unprecedented | Name the precedent it breaks |
| exceptional / exceptionally | Cite what makes it an exception |

| sophisticated | Describe the sophistication |
| instrumental | Say what role it played |
| world-class / state-of-the-art / best-in-class | Cite a benchmark |
| valuable | Say why or for whom |

| vital | Same |

## Crypto and web3 boilerplate

LLM filler that clusters in crypto, web3, and DePIN copy. Flag any of these at one use in public writing:

- "emerging sector"
- "the integration of X with Y"
- "the intersection of X and Y"
- "community-driven"
- "long-term sustainability"
- "user engagement" (as filler)
- "decentralized compute"
- "sustainable reward emissions"
- "tokenized incentive structures"
- "designed for long-term [X]"

Cluster rule. Three or more different boilerplate phrases in one piece flag even at one use each. That shape is an LLM varying its own boilerplate. Also watch "stark contrast" and "stark reminder", which read doubly bad next to Starknet branding.

## Banned sentence openers (chatbot tics)

Chatbot courtesy openers. Cut entirely, no exceptions:

- "Certainly,"
- "Absolutely,"
- "Of course,"
- "Sure,"
- "Indeed,"
- "Great question,"
- "Excellent point,"
- "You're absolutely right,"
- "I'd be happy to"
- "As an AI" / "As a language model"

Emphasis-adverb openers. One precise use may be fine, and repeated use in a short piece signals the writer is announcing emphasis instead of earning it:

- "Moreover,"
- "Furthermore,"
- "Additionally,"
- "Notably,"
- "Importantly,"
- "Interestingly,"
- "Surprisingly,"
- "Overall," (as a paragraph starter)
- "Firstly, … Secondly, … Thirdly,"

In neutral prose, do not open a paragraph with "So" or a sentence with "Look,". A supplied casual voice may keep both.

## Banned phrases (full list)

**Throat-clearing:**
- "Here's the thing"
- "Here's what/this/that/why"
- "Here's the kicker" / "the wild part" / "the crazy part"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It turns out" (keep when reporting a genuine discovery, "it turns out the bottleneck was DNS", cut as a cold opener)
- "The real X is"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The truth is"
- "I'll say it again"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "Can we talk about"
- "This is where X comes in"
- "Buckle up"
- "In a nutshell"
- "Let me explain." (as a one-line follow to a bold claim)

**Emphasis crutches:**
- "Full stop." / "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "Read that again."
- "Make no mistake"
- "This matters because"
- "Here's why that matters"
- "I promise" / "They exist, I promise"
- "creeps in"

**Filler/hedging:**
- "It's worth noting that"
- "It is important to note that"
- "In terms of"
- "The reality is"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "In a world where"
- "A real problem"
- "A real opportunity"
- "The real issue is"
- "In today's [X]"
- "In an era where"
- "At this point in time"
- "In the event that"
- "Has the ability to"
- "Could potentially possibly"
- "Might have some"

**Generic conclusions:**
- "The future looks bright"
- "Only time will tell"
- "One thing is certain"
- "As we move forward"
- "Exciting times lie ahead"
- "Will / could become one of the most [adjective] [nouns]" (replace with a falsifiable prediction, not a vaguer sentence)

**Closers/openers:**
- "I hope this helps"
- "Let me know if…"
- "Feel free to reach out"
- "Let's dive in"
- "Without further ado"
- "In this article we'll explore"
- "Let me walk you through"
- "In conclusion"
- "In summary"
- "To summarize"

**Persuasive authority tropes:**
- "The real question is"
- "At its core"
- "What really matters"
- "Fundamentally," (as an opener or trope — mid-sentence technical degree uses follow the adverb rule in references/patterns.md)
- "The deeper issue"
- "The heart of the matter"
- "In reality"

**Vague endorsement:**
- "worth reading"
- "worth checking out"
- "worth a look"
- "worth exploring"
- "worth your time"

**Vague attributions:**
- "Experts believe"
- "Studies show"
- "Research suggests"
- "Industry observers note"
- "Industry leaders agree"
- "Some critics argue"
- "Several sources"

**Cutoff disclaimers:**
- "As of my last update"
- "Up to my training cutoff"
- "While specific details are limited"
- "Based on available information"
- "I don't have access to real-time data"

**Reasoning-chain leakage:**
- "Let me think step by step"
- "Breaking this down"
- "Working through this logically"
- "To approach this systematically"
- "Step 1:"
- "Here's my thought process"

**Meta-commentary:**
- "Plot twist:"
- "Spoiler:"
- "Hint:"
- "You already know this, but"
- "But that's another post"
- "As we'll see"
- "I want to explore"
- "Dressed up as"

**Email boilerplate:**
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "As per my last email"
- "Please don't hesitate to reach out"
- "I recently had the pleasure of"
